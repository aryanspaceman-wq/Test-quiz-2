Why this study matters

Energy drinks are everywhere in teenage life: at shops, in feeds, during exams and late-night gaming sessions. Yet we know surprisingly little about what actually influences the choices teenagers in Qatar make about them.

The question

This project asks a single, carefully scoped question: which factors are associated with teenage energy drink consumption? We look at age and location, proximity to shops, peer influence, social media exposure, gaming and studying routines, motivations, and awareness of caffeine and health effects, keeping each of these separate so the data can speak for itself.

Research principles

Anonymous by design

No names, school names, exact locations, phone numbers, or social media accounts. Every response is analysed only in aggregate.

Associations, not causation

We measure how factors relate to consumption. We do not claim that any single factor causes someone to consume energy drinks.

Scientific neutrality

Questions use neutral wording and validated-style scales. We avoid labelling participants as fixed personality types.

Honest statistics

We report denominators, missing data, and confidence intervals. Small groups are suppressed to protect privacy. No fabricated results.

UN SDG 12

Connected to UN SDG 12

Responsible Consumption and Production is about doing more and better with less. Understanding why teenagers consume energy drinks is a precondition for informed choices, through clearer labels, better awareness of advertising, and honest conversation, rather than glamourising or stigmatising the drinks.

What we will not do

  • Diagnose personalities or predict who consumes energy drinks.
  • Ask for social media feeds, usernames, messages, or screenshots.
  • Provide medical advice or claim these results represent all teenagers in Qatar.
  • Make energy drink consumption look glamorous or desirable.
